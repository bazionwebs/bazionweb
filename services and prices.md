Any project involving two or more of the following should normally be treated as a custom application rather than a website:

* Authentication
* User accounts
* Multiple user roles
* Database-driven workflows
* Dashboards
* Complex payments
* Booking engine
* CRM
* Inventory
* Reporting
* API integrations
* Automated workflows
* Complex administrative interface

---

## 4. Discovery

Custom projects should begin with a discovery phase.

Discovery should produce:

* Requirements
* User roles
* Workflows
* Feature list
* Technical architecture
* Database requirements
* Integration requirements
* Project milestones
* Estimated development effort

---

## 5. Change Requests

A change request includes:

* New page outside approved scope
* New feature
* New integration
* New workflow
* Additional user role
* Major design change
* Additional functionality
* Requirement introduced after approval

Change requests must be priced before implementation.

---

## 6. Revisions

A revision changes already-approved work.

A new feature is not a revision.

---

## 7. Third-Party Costs

Third-party costs must never be silently absorbed.

Examples:

* Domains
* Premium plugins
* APIs
* SMS
* Email platforms
* Payment services
* Cloud services
* Stock photography
* Premium fonts
* External SaaS

---

## 8. Hosting

Always distinguish:

**Infrastructure cost**

from

**BazionWeb managed hosting service.**

The latter may include:

* Configuration
* Monitoring
* Backups
* Security
* DNS
* Support
* Migration
* Maintenance

---

## 9. Maintenance

Maintenance packages must define a monthly service allocation.

Never offer unlimited development without a fair-use clause.

---

## 10. Client Ownership

Unless otherwise contracted:

The client owns:

* Domain
* Website content
* Business data
* Customer data
* Brand assets

Source-code ownership and licensing must be explicitly defined for custom software.

---

## 11. Payment

No development begins until the agreed deposit is received.

For milestone projects, each milestone should have:

1. Deliverable
2. Acceptance criteria
3. Payment amount
4. Approval point

---

## 12. Launch

Final production deployment should normally occur after:

* Final payment
* Client acceptance
* Required credentials received
* Content approval
* Domain/DNS access confirmed
* Third-party services configured

---

## 13. Support

Included support covers defects and agreed implementation issues.

It does not automatically include:

* New features
* New pages
* New integrations
* New content campaigns
* Marketing
* SEO campaigns
* Third-party outages

---

## 14. International Projects

International projects are priced in USD.

Do not simply convert Kenyan prices into USD.

International proposals should include:

* More formal discovery
* Written scope
* Clear milestones
* Professional documentation
* QA
* Handover documentation
* Defined support period
* International payment terms

---

## 15. Rush Projects

Rush work may attract:

**+25% to +50%**

depending on schedule and resource requirements.

Rush availability is never guaranteed.

---

## 16. Pricing Validity

Standard quotations should remain valid for:

**30 days**

unless otherwise stated.

Third-party costs may change before project commencement.

---

## 17. Minimum Margin Rule

Every project must be reviewed internally for:

* Labour
* Infrastructure
* Third-party costs
* Project management
* Risk
* Support
* Profit

Never accept a project merely because the client agrees to the price.

The project must remain commercially viable.

# 35. One final strategic recommendation

I would **not launch all of this at once on the BazionWeb website**.

The public navigation should be very simple:

**Services**

* Websites
* E-Commerce
* Custom Applications
* Hosting
* Maintenance

**Pricing**

* Kenya
* International

**Industries**

* Businesses
* Churches & Ministries
* Schools & Institutions
* NGOs
* Professionals
* Startups

**Portfolio**

**About**

**Contact**

Then the pricing page presents only the packages relevant to that customer.

This gives BazionWeb a much more mature appearance than a site that simply says *"Website from KSh 10,000."*

And importantly, the current market evidence supports having **both a productized low/mid-tier offering and a custom high-end path**: Kenyan providers publicly range from KSh 15k starter sites through KSh 80k e-commerce/custom offerings, while Nairobi agency benchmarks can be several multiples higher. ([Prime Code Technologies][1])

**The pricing architecture above is therefore the structure I would use as BazionWeb's baseline 2026 price book—not as a claim that every project must be sold at those exact numbers.**
